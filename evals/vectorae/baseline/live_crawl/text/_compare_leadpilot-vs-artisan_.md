# Vectorae vs Artisan, and vs hiring an SDR

**Short answer:** Artisan sells Ava, an autonomous AI BDR that sources, writes, follows up and handles replies on its own. Vectorae is an AI outbound sales tool for agencies and B2B service firms: it researches your market, finds and verifies business contacts, writes one-to-one emails, sends on a schedule you approve each day, and books meetings. The real difference is control: Vectorae waits for your approval every day and never replies to prospects for you, while Ava is designed to run without you.

Written by Vectorae's founders. We make Vectorae, so weigh this accordingly; each section says where the other tool is the better choice.Last checked: 2026-10-16.

## When to choose Vectorae

- You run an agency or B2B service firm of roughly 5 to 50 people, and the founder or owner does the selling.
- You want the market research, contact finding and email writing done for you, not a tool you operate.
- You want to approve each day's sending before anything goes out, and you answer replies yourself.
- Your firm's reputation depends on a human voice in conversations: agencies, consultancies, professional services.
- You want to pay only for booked meetings ($179 each) or a flat monthly price per account.

## When to choose Artisan

- You want maximum autonomy, including AI follow-ups and objection handling in replies.
- You want a large built-in contact database, a dialer and multichannel reach today.
- You value a larger, funded vendor with an established brand.

## When to hire an SDR instead

A human SDR can call, handle live objections and build relationships, which no tool replaces. The Bridge Group's 2025 SDR report (351 B2B companies) gives a median SDR on-target earnings of $80K, an average ramp time of 3.0 months, an average tenure of 1.9 years, and 60% of SDRs at quota ( [Bridge Group](https://www.bridgegroupinc.com/research/2025-sdr-models-metrics-report-the-bridge-group)). Hiring makes sense once you know which segment buys and have someone to manage the hire.
